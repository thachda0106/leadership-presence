# Lesson 16 — Giving Feedback

> Program: Leadership Presence & Body Language · Phase 4 — Leadership Presence (4/8)
> Delivered: 2026-09-20 · Builds on: L12 (Listening), L15 (Disagreeing), L3 (Voice), L1 (Posture)

---

## 1. Core concept

**Feedback is a gift — but only if it's delivered in a box the other person can open.** The goal is not to vent or to be right; it's to change one behavior while keeping the relationship intact.

The **SBI frame** — the whole skill in three sentences:

| Step | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **S — Situation** | When and where | "In Tuesday's sprint review…" |
| **B — Behavior** | What they actually did (observable, video-camera facts) | "…the estimate for the CSV export was cut from 3 days to 1 without telling the team." |
| **I — Impact** | What happened because of it | "…so Thursday's plan was built on a wrong date, and the team had to replan." |

Core rules:

- **Facts, not labels.** "The report was 2 days late" (fact) ✅ · "You're unreliable" (label) ❌
- **One topic per conversation.** Dumping five issues = zero addressed.
- **Private for correction, public for praise.** Public criticism humiliates; praise in public multiplies. In JP culture this is *essential* — face is everything.
- **Ask before sensitive ones:** *"May I give you some feedback?"* — the question opens the door; people accept a door they opened.
- **Positive ratio:** for every corrective conversation, build up several genuine positive ones. Correction should be the rare coin, not the currency.

## 2. Why it matters

1. **Teams that don't get feedback don't grow** — issues silently repeat, and resentment builds on both sides.
2. **Your job as a leader is partly "making the invisible visible"** — people genuinely can't see their own blind spots.
3. **Giving feedback badly is worse than giving none** — it reads as an attack, and the behavior stays.
4. **SBI makes you calm** — with a structure, you don't need anger to make a point; the facts carry it.

## 3. Psychology / evidence

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Specific, task-focused feedback improves performance; vague or person-focused feedback can backfire | **Well-supported** (Kluger & DeNisi meta-analysis: ~1/3 of feedback studies showed *negative* effects — structure matters) |
| Public criticism damages status and relationships | **Well-supported** (status research; JP face culture) |
| The SBI / situation-behavior-impact frame | **Reasonable practical advice** — a field-tested coaching tool |
| "The sandwich method (praise–criticism–praise)" | **Popular but weak** — often confuses the receiver ("was that good or bad?"); direct SBI is clearer |
| "Always end feedback with a question" | Reasonable — invites dialogue, but don't force it |

## 4. Common mistakes

| Mistake | What it does | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Vague: "You need to be more proactive" | Nobody can act on it | SBI with observable behavior |
| Labeling the person | Defensive, hurt | Facts only |
| Public correction | Humiliation, quiet resentment | Private, always (esp. JP) |
| The sandwich | Confusion | SBI, direct and kind |
| Dumping everything | Nothing lands | One topic |
| Delaying 3 weeks | Sounds petty | Close to the event (1–3 days) |
| "You always / you never" | Absolutes, accusation | "This time…" |
| Only praise or only critique | Erodes trust both ways | Both, honestly |

## 5. Workplace examples

- **Corrective — missed deadline:** "In this sprint (S), the login page shipped 3 days late and the team only learned about it in the review (B). The release slipped and we had to cut features (I). What happened, and what can we change next sprint?" — ends with a question, not a verdict.
- **Corrective — silent teammate:** "In Monday's design meeting (S), you didn't share your API concerns (B). The team chose the schema without your input and we'll pay for it later (I). I'd like to hear your view — what do you see?"
- **Positive — public:** ("Praise in public:") "In the retro, Lâm caught the cache bug before it hit production — that saved the release. I want everyone to know how that review worked." (Specific, public, names the behavior.)
- **JP context:** correction is nearly always private and *indirect*: "In the last release, the estimate was a bit tight — next time, could we check together before committing? I want to help." (Suggests, offers help, saves face.)
- **Mentoring (A Thiên / Lâm context):** "Your PR was great. One thing: the error handling reuses a pattern we removed last month (S/B). It would be safer to use the new one (I). Want to pair on it?"

## 6. Before vs After

| 😟 Harsh / vague | 😌 Calm / confident |
|---|---|
| "You're always late with everything." | "This sprint, the login page shipped 3 days late (SBI)." |
| "You need to be more proactive." | "In Tuesday's review you stayed quiet (B) — your input would have changed the decision (I)." |
| Says it in front of the team | Says it in private, calmly |
| Dumps five issues | One topic, one conversation |
| "Good job… but…" sandwich | Specific SBI, honest and warm |

## 7. Practical drills (measurable)

1. **The label-to-fact converter — 10 min.** Write 5 feedback labels ("lazy", "sloppy", "unreliable", "great", "careless") → convert each into an SBI statement with observable behavior.
2. **The SBI writer — 15 min.** Write 2 real, current feedback messages (one positive, one corrective) using SBI exactly: Situation → Behavior → Impact.
3. **The 60-second delivery — 10 min.** Record yourself delivering one SBI message in under 60 seconds. Replay: did it stay factual? Did you label anyone?
4. **The door-opener drill — 5 min.** Practice the ask: "May I give you some feedback?" / "Can I share something I noticed?" — say each 5 times until it sounds natural.
5. **The positive-bank habit — all day.** Today, catch one person doing something right and tell them, specifically, within the hour. Count how many you gave.

## 8. Real-world challenge (today)

## 10. Coaching exercise — ✋ STOP HERE

Record **60 seconds**: deliver corrective feedback to a teammate about this situation — *"In last week's stand-ups, they didn't mention the DB migration delay, so the team planned on the wrong date."* Use full SBI and a warm tone.

Answer **4 questions**:

1. Did you stay on facts (S → B → I)?
2. Did any label slip in ("lazy", "careless")?
3. Did you end with an open question?
4. Rate your **feedback delivery** 1–5.
