# Lesson 14 — Speaking in Meetings

> Program: Leadership Presence & Body Language · Phase 4 — Leadership Presence (2/8)
> Delivered: 2026-09-20 · Builds on: L3 (Voice), L10 (Silence), L9 (Gestures), L13 (Entry)

---

## 1. Core concept

**A meeting contribution is a product — it needs structure.** The people who sound like leaders are not the most talkative; they are the most **structured**. Speak less, but say things that are *shaped*.

The **P.R.E.A. frame** — the whole technique in four parts:

| Step | What you do | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **P — Point** | Say the conclusion FIRST | "We should move the deploy to Thursday." |
| **R — Reason** | One sentence why | "Because Friday has no engineering coverage." |
| **E — Evidence** | One concrete fact | "Last Friday's incident took 4 hours to resolve." |
| **A — Ask** | What you want from the room | "Does anyone see a blocker to Thursday?" |

Two rules on top:

- **One idea per turn.** If you have three ideas, give one now — the other two after people respond. One shaped idea beats three rambling ones.
- **Speak within the first 10 minutes.** The meeting's shape forms early. A leader contributes early (even briefly), then listens (L12).

**The 30-second principle:** if your point needs more than ~30 seconds to make, you haven't found the point yet. Cut it.

## 2. Why it matters

1. **Structured speech is heard as competence.** Two people can say the same content; the one with structure is remembered as "the clear one."
2. **Leadership is visibility.** Quiet competence in a corner is invisible. Speaking well *early and rarely* is the leader's frequency.
3. **It respects the room's time** — and people notice.
4. **Structure reduces your own nerves** — a frame is a rail; you can't derail.

## 3. Psychology / evidence

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Structured vs rambling speech is rated more credible/competent | **Well-supported** (communication research) |
| Speaking early in a meeting shapes perception of involvement | **Reasonable practical advice** |
| The P.R.E.A. / PREP frame | **Reasonable practical advice** — a coaching tool, based on how attention works |
| "Talk as much as possible to look important" | **Wrong** — quantity without structure reads as noise; quality is the signal |

**Attention note:** in a meeting, people's attention is finite and uneven. Your first 30 seconds are worth more than your next 2 minutes. That's why the **point comes first** — if you get cut off, the point is already out.

## 4. Common mistakes

| Mistake | What it does | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Ramping in with context ("So, as we discussed, the thing is…") | Buries the point | Point first, context after |
| Speaking last, or never | Invisible | One contribution in the first 10 minutes |
| Repeating what others said | Noise | Add ONE new fact or angle, or stay silent |
| Interrupting | Aggressive | L10: wait 2 seconds after they finish |
| "Just my two cents…" | Approval-seeking | Own the point: "I think we should…" |
| Three ideas in one turn | Confusion | One idea per turn |
| Checking devices when others speak | Disrespect, absent | L12: listen with your eyes too |

## 5. Workplace examples

- **Stand-up update (30s):** "Point: I'll finish the auth refactor today. Reason: the DB migration is the only blocker left. Evidence: two of three modules are done. Ask: I need a code review by 4pm."
- **Design discussion:** "Point: I think we should reject this approach. Reason: it doubles the queue load. Evidence: the load test at 60% capacity already showed latency spikes. Ask: can we compare it against option B?"
- **Sprint planning:** contribute to the scope decision with P.R.E.A. — then let others talk.
- **JP meetings:** the *structure* is the same, but the *delivery* is softer: "I think we should consider moving the deploy…" — point still first, wrapped in consideration.
- **Interview:** a P.R.E.A. answer to any "walk me through…" question = instant clarity. Example: "Point: I'd start with the API layer. Reason: it's the contract everything depends on. Evidence: in the Umaps monolith… Ask: may I go deeper on the DB?"

## 6. Before vs After

| 😟 Nervous | 😌 Calm / confident |
|---|---|
| "Um, so, I was thinking maybe we could, you know, possibly consider…" | "We should move the deploy to Thursday — Friday has no coverage." |
| Speaks once at minute 35, then hides | Contributes in the first 10 minutes |
| Three rambling ideas | One shaped idea |
| Repeats the last speaker | Adds one new fact or angle |
| "Just my two cents…" | "I think we should…" |

## 7. Practical drills (measurable)

1. **The 30-second cut — 10 min.** Write your last stand-up update as it really happened (however long). Now cut it to 30 seconds using P.R.E.A. Record it. Then cut again to 20 seconds. Notice how the point gets sharper.
2. **The P.R.E.A. builder — 10 min.** Take 3 real topics (the migration, the auth refactor, a bug you're chasing). Build a P.R.E.A. frame for each, in writing, 2 minutes each. Then say them aloud.
3. **The first-10-minutes drill — today.** In every meeting: commit to ONE contribution in the first 10 minutes. Even "I have a question about…" counts. Count how many meetings you hit it.
4. **The one-idea drill — 5 min.** Record yourself explaining one technical decision. Every time you start a new idea, stop. Restart. Keep only ONE idea per 60 seconds.
5. **The question-as-contribution — 5 min.** Convert your next "I disagree" into a structured question: "What would change if the deploy moved to Thursday?" — notice how the room opens.

## 10. Coaching exercise — ✋ STOP HERE

Record **60 seconds**: deliver a real stand-up-style update with the full P.R.E.A. frame — *"Point, reason, evidence, ask"* out loud in that order (on the topic of your choice).

Answer **4 questions**:

1. How many seconds did it take? (Longer than 40 = cut harder.)
2. Did the point come first, or did you ramble in?
3. Did you end with a clear ask?
4. Rate your **structured speech** 1–5.
