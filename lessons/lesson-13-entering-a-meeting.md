# Lesson 13 — Entering a Meeting

> Program: Leadership Presence & Body Language · Phase 4 — Leadership Presence (1/8)
> Delivered: 2026-09-20 · Builds on: L4 (Walking), L1 (Posture), L2 (Breath), L5 (Eye Contact), L6 (Smile)

---

## 1. Core concept

**The first 30 seconds of a meeting set the tone for the next 30 minutes.** Before you say anything, people have already read: are you calm, present, and ready — or rushed, distracted, and low-energy?

The **entry sequence** (from the moment you reach the door):

1. **Arrive on purpose** — stop at the door, brief pause (your L4 arrival pause).
2. **Scan the room** — a slow sweep of faces with a smile and a nod (L5 + L6).
3. **Take your place** — your designated spot, or a spot that lets you see everyone (for the meeting owner: the head of the table / first seat in the room).
4. **Settle** — sit/stand tall (L1), one slow breath (L2). *Do not touch your phone.*
5. **THEN speak** — greeting, or your first contribution. Never talk while walking in.

**On video (your daily reality):** the same sequence compressed — camera at eye level, settle into frame, one breath, greet *by name* the people you can see, then contribute.

**If you lead the meeting:** arrive **first**. Greet each person as they come in with eye contact + name. That single habit makes you the owner of the room before the agenda starts.

## 2. Why it matters

1. **First impressions anchor everything** — once formed, they bias how people hear your later words (the primacy effect).
2. **Rushing in = rushing the whole meeting.** Your entry pace sets the room's pace (L4: people mirror you).
3. **The arrival pause is a status signal, not a delay** — people who can pause before speaking look like they own the moment.
4. **It protects you:** breathing before you start means your first sentence comes out calm (L2 + L3), not breathless.

## 3. Psychology / evidence

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| First impressions form in seconds and anchor later judgment (primacy) | **Well-supported** (impression-formation research) |
| People mirror the energy they see — a calm entrance calms the room | **Well-supported** (behavioral contagion) |
| The "30-second entry protocol" | **Reasonable practical advice** — a coaching sequence, not a law |
| "Make a big dramatic entrance to be remembered" | **Wrong for this program** — theater, not presence |

**JP context (important):** the meeting room has a hierarchy. Wait for the host to indicate your seat; greet with a nod/bow proportional to rank; do **not** sit before the host, and **do not** start talking before the host opens. In a video call with JP managers: wait your turn, greet politely, keep the entry brief. Respectful entry = calm entry.

## 4. Common mistakes

| Mistake | What it says | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Rushing in, breathless, talking while walking | Anxious, subordinate | Arrival pause at the door |
| Entering while checking the phone | Not invested | Phone away, face the room |
| Slumping into the seat silently | Low energy, absent | Settle tall + greet |
| Sitting as far from the action as possible | Hiding, uninvolved | Take a real spot in the flow |
| Joining late and over-apologizing | Collapses status | One brief "apologies for the late start", then settle |
| On video: joining with camera off + no greeting | Invisible, distant | Greet on entry, camera on if possible |

## 5. Workplace examples

- **Daily stand-up:** walk in calm, settle, your first sentence is your update — not a breathless "sorry, too many meetings".
- **Sprint planning:** as the meeting owner — arrive first, greet each person by name, then open with the goal.
- **1-on-1 with your manager:** enter, settle, one breath — then the conversation starts on *your* calm, not your rush.
- **Interview (job search):** the interviewer's first read of you is the entry — walk in steady, settle, greet with name + smile + eye contact. This literally shapes everything after.
- **Incident war-room:** walk in slow, breathe, then speak — the room instantly drops a notch.
- **Video call with JP team:** settle into frame, small nod, "Good morning, everyone" — then contribute when the flow reaches you.

## 6. Before vs After

| 😟 Nervous | 😌 Calm / confident |
|---|---|
| Rushes through the door, breathless | Arrival pause at the door |
| Enters looking at the phone | Enters scanning faces |
| Slumps into the seat | Settles tall, one breath |
| Starts talking immediately, apologizing | Greets, settles, then speaks |
| Sits in the corner | Takes a real place in the room |
| On video: fumbling, camera off | Settled in frame, greeted by name |

## 7. Practical drills (measurable)

1. **The door-to-seat sequence — 5 min.** Walk to a door, pause, open, enter, scan (3 faces/nod), walk to a chair, settle tall, one breath — then greet. ×5 reps. Time each: target a steady, unhurried 5–8 seconds from door to seat.
2. **The name-greeting drill — 5 min.** With 3 photos of teammates: enter, look at each, nod + "Good morning, [Name]" with a small smile. This wires the greeting into your entry.
3. **The video-settle drill — 10 min.** Record yourself "joining a call": camera at eye level, settle into frame, one breath, "Good morning, everyone" — 3 reps. Watch: do you fidget or settle?
4. **The 3-breath pre-entry — all day.** Before every meeting today: 3 slow breaths at the door (L2). Count how many times you remembered.
