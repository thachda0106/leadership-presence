# Lesson 4 — Confident Walking

> Program: Leadership Presence \& Body Language · Phase 1 — Foundation (4/4 · FINALE)

## 1\. Core concept

**Walking is posture in motion.** Everything from Lessons 1–3 is on display while you move — and people form their first impression of you *before* you speak, from the way you arrive.

Three moments matter:

1. **The approach** — how you walk toward a room, a person, or a stage.
2. **The arrival** — reaching your spot, stopping, settling.
3. **The moment before speaking** — the pause where you breathe and then talk.

**The arrival pause is the key move:** walk in → stop → settle (plant both feet, L1) → one breath (L2) → THEN speak. People who talk while walking into a room look rushed. People who arrive, settle, and *then* speak look like they own the moment — this single pause changes everything.

**Mental model:** a calm walk is *steady, unhurried, purposeful.* Nervous walking is fast (anxious), head-down (hiding), or stiff (tense). Confidence is not speed — it's steadiness.

## 2\. Why it matters

1. **You are seen before you are heard.** The walk is the first 5 seconds — meetings, interviews, presentations, incident rooms.
2. **Speed is a signal people read instantly:** rushing = anxious · dragging = low energy · **steady = composed**.
3. **The arrival pause sets up everything else** — it re-plants your posture, re-fills your breath, and re-sets your voice. L4 is where L1–L3 become a habit.
4. **Other people mirror your pace.** Walk calmly into a war room → the room follows. Walk in frantic → the room stays frantic.

## 3\. Psychology / evidence

|Claim|Status|
|-|-|
|People reliably judge emotion, energy, and approachability from the way someone walks (gait)|**Well-supported** (gait-perception studies)|
|Head up + relaxed shoulders while moving reduces stress and reads as open|**Reasonable practical advice** (same body–mind link as L1)|
|"Walk very fast / stride like a CEO to look powerful"|**Popular advice — limited evidence, and it often backfires.** Frantic reads as anxious; exaggerated striding reads as performance/dominance. Steady is the signal, not speed|
|"Power-walk into a room to intimidate"|**Wrong for this program** — intimidation is dominance, not confidence|

**Honest note:** there is no magic "walk of power." There is a *settled* walk — even pace, level head, eyes up — and it reads as calm and trustworthy, which is exactly the presence you're building.

## 4\. Common mistakes

|Mistake|What it says|Fix|
|-|-|-|
|Walking too fast|Anxious, late, stressed|Steady even pace (drill 2)|
|Head down, eyes on floor/phone|Hiding, low status|Eyes up at head height (drill 4)|
|Talking while entering / walking in|Rushed, no gravity|Arrival pause: stop → breathe → speak|
|Stiff arms, no swing / flailing arms|Tense / nervous energy|Relaxed, natural swing|
|Shuffling feet / dragging|Low energy, careless|Lift feet, even steps|
|Rushing straight to your seat and starting|No moment of arrival|Settle, breathe, then start|

## 5\. Workplace examples

* **Entering a meeting room:** walk in steady, arrive at your spot, pause one second, breathe, greet — *then* sit.
* **Joining a video call (your daily reality):** same principle — when you speak first, pause before the first word rather than jumping in.
* **Walking to the whiteboard / front to present:** the walk is part of the presentation. Cross calmly, turn to face the room, settle, breathe, then open.
* **Approaching a colleague for a 1-on-1 or difficult conversation:** a steady, unhurried approach signals *"this is calm, intentional, not a confrontation."*
* **Entering an interview room (or the video call):** the interviewer sees your composure in the first 5 seconds.
* **Incident war-room:** walk in steady + breathe — the room takes its pace from you.
* **Japanese enterprise context:** corridors call for a steady, moderate pace. When passing a manager, a brief pause and a slight nod is the respectful acknowledgment. Don't rush past — and never walk in talking fast.

## 6\. Before vs After

|😟 Nervous version|😌 Calm / confident version|
|-|-|
|Fast, almost jogging, breathless|Steady, unhurried pace|
|Head down at the floor / phone|Head level, eyes up at head height|
|Eyes darting, scanning the floor|Glancing ahead, meeting people's eyes as you arrive|
|Talks while walking into the room|Arrives → settles → one breath → then speaks|
|Stiff arms|Relaxed natural swing|
|Slumps as soon as sitting down|Stays tall into the seat|

## 7\. Practical drills (measurable)

1. **The 10-stride check — 2 min.** Walk 10 strides with head level and eyes on a fixed point at head height ahead of you. Count how many times your eyes dropped to the floor. Target: 0.
2. **The pace test — 5 min.** Record yourself walking 20 steps. Count the seconds. Now walk the same 20 steps \~15% slower and re-time. Feel the difference in your body — slower is calmer, and other people read it that way.
3. **The door drill — 3 min.** Walk to a doorway, stop, pause 1 second, take one breath, THEN say a greeting ("Good morning"). Repeat 5×. This trains the arrival pause — the single most powerful move in this lesson.
4. **The eyes-up walk — all day.** Every time you walk through your office/hallway/street: eyes at head height, glance at doorways and faces ahead — not the floor, not your phone.
5. **The entrance rehearsal — 5 min.** Practice the full sequence on camera: walk in, cross to a spot, turn to face the room, settle, breathe, then deliver your stand-up opening. 3 reps — watch the difference between rep 1 and rep 3.
