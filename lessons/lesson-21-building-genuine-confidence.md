# Lesson 21 — Building Genuine Confidence

> Program: Leadership Presence & Body Language · Phase 5 — Authenticity (1/5)
> Delivered: 2026-09-22 · Builds on: everything in L1–L20 · L19 (pressure), L18 (decisions), L17 (criticism), L14 (speaking in meetings)

---

## 1. Core concept

**Genuine confidence is not a feeling you wait for — it is trust you build with evidence.**

Most people get the order wrong. They think: *become confident → then act.* But the real order is:
**act → collect evidence → confidence follows.** Confidence is the *result* of doing hard things and noticing they went fine.

Three ingredients build it:

1. **Evidence of mastery.** Small wins you can point to: a review you led, an incident you stayed calm in, a feature you shipped. Confidence is a log, not a mood.
2. **Self-trust.** You keep promises to yourself. If you say "I'll prepare 20 minutes for this meeting," you do it. Each kept promise raises your trust in yourself.
3. **An internal reference point.** You compare yourself to *yesterday's you*, not to the senior architect's highlight reel. Other people's highlights have no editing in your head, so they always win.

Genuine confidence is **quiet**. It speaks normally, asks questions, and admits "I don't know yet" (L24 will go deep into this). It does not need to be loud, and it never needs to dominate (confidence ≠ dominance).

## 2. Why it matters

1. **Fake confidence collapses** the moment it is tested. Genuine confidence survives mistakes — it treats them as data.
2. **People follow calm certainty.** In an incident, the team needs a person who says "we'll fix this" without pretending everything is fine.
3. **It protects your judgment.** Without it, you copy the loudest opinion in the room (L22: approval-seeking).
4. **Interviews.** Every interviewer is scanning for one thing: *does this person trust themselves under pressure?*

## 3. Psychology / evidence

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Mastery experiences are the strongest source of confidence (self-efficacy) | **Well-supported** (Bandura's self-efficacy research) |
| Doing a feared thing repeatedly lowers the fear next time (habituation) | **Well-supported** (exposure research) |
| People overestimate how much others watch and judge them (spotlight effect) | **Well-supported** (Gilovich et al.) |
| "Fake it till you make it" as a long-term strategy | **Popular claim — limited evidence** — can *increase* impostor feelings when the gap between act and self feels large |
| "Power posing raises confidence hormones" | **Popular claim — limited evidence** (failed replication; original researcher walked back the hormone claim) |
| Impostor feelings are common in high achievers | **Well-supported** — and they usually mean "my standards grew," not "I'm a fraud" |

**Key insight:** nervousness and confidence are not opposites. Confident people feel nervous; they just act anyway. The goal is not an empty stomach — it is a calm voice *while* the stomach flips (L3, L19).

## 4. Common mistakes

| Mistake | What it does | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Waiting to feel confident before acting | You never feel ready — you stay silent for years | Act first, collect evidence after |
| Comparing yourself to the loudest or most senior person | They have 10 years of logs you can't see | Compare to yesterday's you |
| Requiring certainty before speaking | You speak last or not at all | Speak with 70% confidence + honest limits |
| Loudness / interrupting as confidence | Reads as dominance, lowers trust | Quiet, structured, plain speaking |
| Reading impostor feelings as proof of fraud | You shrink instead of growing | Reframe: it means your bar moved |
| Perfectionism as fuel | One flaw destroys the whole win | Log wins even when imperfect |

## 5. Workplace examples

- **Speaking with the senior architect in the room:** everyone waits for his opinion. You speak first: *"Before we go deep — the queue design assumes events arrive in order. Is that true for the producer?"* One good question is evidence. Later, log it.
- **Owning a mistake in the incident review:** *"I introduced this change. My check missed the race condition. Here's what I'm changing tonight, and here's how we catch it in review next time."* Owning = confidence. Deflecting = fear.
- **Estimating honestly under pressure (L18):** *"I don't know the exact cost yet — I'll check two things and give you a number by 3pm."* Certainty about the process, honesty about the number.
- **Saying no to the manager:** *"Adding this now means we cut the login fix. I'd recommend keeping the login fix — your call either way."* Standing behind a technical judgment is confidence in action.
- **Interview (L19 applies):** *"I haven't worked with Kafka at scale, but I know the failure modes — here's how I'd design the retry and DLQ."* Honest about the gap, confident about the method.

## 6. Before vs After

| 😟 Low / fake confidence | 😌 Genuine confidence |
|---|---|
| Waits to feel ready | Acts, then collects evidence |
| Stays silent until asked | One good point in the first 10 minutes (L14) |
| "Sorry, I might be wrong, but…" | "My view is X, and here's why." |
| Deflects mistakes | Owns them, then fixes |
| Copies the loudest person | Has an internal reference point |
| Needs applause | Needs progress |

## 7. Practical drills (measurable)

1. **The evidence log — 3 minutes a day.** Write 3 things you did well today (small is fine: "caught a bug in review", "spoke in the stand-up"). One week = 21 entries. This is your confidence log.
2. **Action-first experiment — 1 per week.** Pick one thing you avoid (volunteer to present, ask a question to the senior, offer an estimate). Do it once, then write one line: what happened, what you expected vs. reality. Expectation vs. reality almost always surprises you in a good way.
3. **The 70% rule — 5 minutes.** Say an opinion out loud with 70% confidence and honest limits: *"This is my recommendation — I haven't checked the failure case yet, but the design looks right."* Practise the sentence 3 times.
4. **The internal-comparison check — 1 minute.** Before every meeting, ask: *"Am I comparing to someone else's highlight reel?"* If yes, reset the reference point.
5. **Record 90 seconds** answering: *"What is Umaps, and what is one technical decision you are proud of?"* Listen once. Notice: did you minimize or brag? Genuine confidence does neither.

## 10. Coaching exercise — ✋ STOP HERE

1. Did you speak plainly, or did you brag / minimize?
2. Could you name concrete evidence for each of the three?
3. Rate your **genuine confidence** 1–5 right now (no wrong answer — this is your baseline).
