## 1. Core concept

**A difficult conversation is a test of presence.** The two fears inside it — *the feared outcome* and *the feared loss of status* — make people rush, dodge, or explode. The leader's move is to make the conversation *safe enough to be honest*, then let the structure carry the rest.

The **difficult-conversation structure** — seven moves:

1. **Prepare (before the room).** Write: the topic, the facts (not interpretations), my goal, the next step I want. 5 minutes of preparation beats 30 minutes of regret.
2. **Open plainly.** Name the topic without softening it into fog: *"I want to talk about the missed deadline on the login page."* (Not: "Let's catch up about how things are going, generally…")
3. **Give facts + impact (SBI from L16).** Situation → Behavior → Impact. No character verdicts.
4. **Silence — let them respond.** Ask *"What's your view?"* — then **hold the silence** (L10, 5 full seconds). Most people fill it with the truth. Do not rescue them.
5. **Listen fully (L12).** Paraphrase back: *"So the API docs were the blocker — you didn't know the dates were dependent. Is that right?"*
6. **Find the shared problem, not the blame.** You both want the release to succeed; find where the system failed, then the person issue inside it.
7. **Close with a next step + time.** *"We'll update the dependency list every Monday, and the release moves to Thursday. Agreed?"* A difficult conversation without a next step is a complaint with a ticket number.

**The two intentions check (before you open the door):** Am I doing this to *help* (they grow, the team improves) or to *punish* (prove I'm right)? Only the first one should speak.

## 2. Why it matters

1. **Postponed conversations grow.** The 5-minute talk you avoid becomes the 3-month problem you inherit.
2. **Your team watches how you handle hard moments** — it decides whether they bring you truth or silence.
3. **Difficult conversations are where leadership is *felt*.** Anyone can run a good status meeting; the hard ones separate the title from the person.
4. **For you: delegating, mentoring (A Thiên, Lâm), deadline problems, and JP face-sensitivity** — this structure protects everyone's dignity while still doing the work.

## 3. Psychology / evidence

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Avoiding difficult conversations increases resentment and team dysfunction | **Well-supported** (conflict research) |
| "Starting with facts, not judgments" keeps the other person out of fight-or-flight | **Well-supported** (threat response in conversations; crucial-conversations literature) |
| Allowing silence after a question gets more honest answers | **Reasonable practical advice** (interview research: wait-time effects are real) |
| "Emotional hijacking" — once triggered, reasoning degrades; structure prevents triggering | **Reasonable + popular** (Goleman's framing; the underlying stress response is well-supported) |
| "Always confront immediately and hard" | **Wrong for JP culture** — face-saving matters; directness is calibrated, not absolute |

**JP context — critical:** direct confrontation is the LAST tool, not the first. The ladder: private → soft frame → "I may be wrong" → suggestion → (rarely) direct statement. Praise and criticism both travel indirectly. The structure above stays the same; the *shell* softens: *"I might be overthinking this, but I want to make sure we're aligned on the login page estimate…"*

## 4. Common mistakes

| Mistake | What it does | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Ambushing ("got a minute?") → diving in angry | Fight-or-flight, defensiveness | Name the topic + set the frame first |
| Doing it in public or in the hallway | Humiliation (JP: unforgivable) | Private, seated, unhurried |
| Making it about character | Attack | Facts + impact only |
| Filling the silence after your question | You answer yourself | Hold 5 seconds (L10) |
| Playing therapist or judge | Role confusion | Structure: facts → listen → next step |
| Ending without a next step | Nothing changes | Close with action + time |
| Avoiding it entirely | It grows | The 5-minute prep sheet as the antidote |

## 5. Workplace examples

- **Missed deadline (team member):** *"I want to talk about the login page landing 3 days late (topic). In the sprint review it wasn't mentioned until I asked (fact). The team planned on the wrong date and features were cut (impact). What's your view?"* → silence → listen → agree: *"Whoever sees a blocking risk raises it in the daily stand-up — and dates get a warning flag. Can we make that the rule?"*
- **Conflict between two teammates:** *"I want to understand what happened in the review — from both of you. First you, then you. My goal is a workflow both can live with."* (Structure, neutrality, shared problem.)
- **Saying no to the manager (scope):** *"I need to push back on the added scope. Here's what we're committed to, here's the cost of adding this, and here's what I'd cut to make room. Your call — but let's decide today."* (Respectful, factual, timeboxed — L18.)
- **JP context (underperformer):** *"I might be wrong, but I noticed the estimates have been tight lately. I'd like to support you — could we look at the planning together?"* (Soft shell, real substance, face intact.)
- **The conversation you've been postponing (pick YOUR one):** the prep sheet is the same for all of them.

## 6. Before vs After

| 😟 Avoidant / explosive | 😌 Calm / confident |
|---|---|
| Postpones for weeks | 5-minute prep, then opens |
| "So, how's everything…?" | "I want to talk about the missed deadline." |
| Accuses: "You don't care about the team." | Facts + impact (SBI) |
| Asks, then answers himself | Holds the silence, listens |
| Ends with "think about it" | Ends with a next step + time |
| In JP: blurts it out directly | Same substance, soft frame, face saved |

## 7. Practical drills (measurable)

1. **The prep sheet — 15 min.** Take your real pending difficult conversation. Write 5 fields in 5 minutes: topic / facts / my goal / their likely view / next step. Timed — prep is a habit, not a ritual.
2. **The opener lab — 10 min.** Write 3 openers for the same conversation: (a) plain, (b) soft/JP, (c) urgent. Say all 3 — pick the one that keeps your voice calm (L3).
3. **The 5-second silence — practice with a friend.** Ask a real question, then hold silence for 5 full seconds (count in your head). Notice how much they say. Do it 3 times.
4. **The paraphrase loop — 5 min.** For 3 imagined responses ("it wasn't my fault, the API docs were wrong"), build the paraphrase: "So the docs were the blocker — is that right?" Say them aloud.
5. **The next-step closer — 5 min.** For the same 3 cases, write the closing: action + time + ownership shift ("we'll", not "you'll").
