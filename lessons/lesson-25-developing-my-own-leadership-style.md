## 1. Core concept

**Your leadership style is not a copy of anyone — it is your strengths, values, and signature moves working as one machine.**

After 24 lessons you have a full toolbox. Style is what happens when you stop carrying the toolbox and start using only the tools that are *you*. Three parts:

1. **Your strengths** — what people already come to you for (clear explanations, calm under pressure, honest estimates, careful review).
2. **Your values** — your non-negotiables: e.g. "I tell the truth about estimates", "I protect my team from blame", "I respect the JP face".
3. **Your signature moves** — the 3–4 behaviors that are unmistakably you: maybe you always clarify scope first, maybe you always hold the silence after a hard question, maybe you speak first within 10 minutes (L14).

**Authenticity = consistency.** You show the same person in the stand-up, the incident, the 1:1, and the interview. That consistency is exactly why authenticity cannot be faked — a costume is inconsistent the moment pressure arrives, and people feel the seam.

Style is also a **filter that makes decisions faster**: "how would I handle this?" becomes "this is how I handle this." No more re-deciding the same situations from scratch (L18).

## 2. Why it matters

1. **People follow people, not titles.** A recognizable, consistent style is why a team trusts you before you say anything.
2. **The toolbox alone is tiring.** Trying all 24 tools at once is overload; a style tells you which 8 are automatic and which 1–3 you still practice.
3. **It protects you from copying wrong models** — the aggressive senior, the fake-positive consultant, the silent autocrat (confidence ≠ dominance, remember).
4. **Interviews and your new job search:** "What kind of leader are you?" is a real question. A style statement answers it in one sentence.
5. **JP context:** your style lives *inside* the polite shell — quiet, calm, clear, face-protecting, decisive. That combination is what a Japanese enterprise reads as "leader".

## 3. Psychology / evidence

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Self-awareness + consistent values + balanced processing build trust (authentic leadership theory) | **Well-supported construct** (Avolio & Gardner; effects on followers reasonable) |
| Behavioral consistency is a base of credibility | **Reasonable** (credibility research — consistency over time builds trust) |
| Leading from strengths correlates with engagement | **Reasonable** (Gallup strengths research; some methodological critique, but practical) |
| Copying a role model wholesale works short-term, erodes authenticity long-term | **Reasonable practical advice** (identification vs. internalization in learning) |
| "There is one best leadership style" | **Wrong** — best is *a fit*: your strengths × your context (JP enterprise, engineer-led team) |

**Key insight (L21 again):** style is not a costume you assemble. It is a *selection* — you choose which of your real behaviors deserve to become automatic. You are not inventing a persona; you are deciding which parts of yourself get promoted.

## 4. Common mistakes

| Mistake | What it does | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Copying one role model wholesale | You become a weak second copy | Extract 2 things, leave the rest |
| Trying all 24 tools every day | Overload; nothing becomes automatic | The 8-automatic / 3-practice audit |
| Building a "professional mask" | People feel the seam; trust drops | Style = selection of the real, not a mask |
| Thinking style is fixed forever | You stop growing | Re-audit every 6 months |
| Ignoring context (JP, your team) | Your strength reads as rudeness | Style has a shell; substance stays |
| No style statement | You answer "what kind of leader are you?" with a ramble | One sentence, rehearsed |

## 5. Workplace examples

- **Your signature, concrete:** *"I clarify first, then commit."* You open every engagement with the scope question, then you commit loudly. That one move, repeated, IS your style.
- **1:1 with A Thiên (ownership goal):** your mentoring style might be *"I ask, you decide first."* You give him the first move in every decision — his ownership, your patience. A signature move.
- **1:1 with Lâm (onboarding):** your style might be *"small steps + fast feedback"* — you never dump; you give one task, one review, one correction. Also a signature move.
- **Incident follow-up (L19, L23):** your style: *"facts first, voice slow, blame last."* People describe you with that sentence, and that's the proof a style exists.
- **Interview answer (L24 + this lesson):** *"I lead by making the path clear — I clarify the goal, decide with a deadline, and stay calm in the uncertainty. In my team, people know where we're going and that I'll take the blame so they can take the credit."* One honest sentence.

## 7. Practical drills (measurable)

1. **The strengths inventory — 10 minutes.** Write answers to 3 questions: (a) What do people at work come to ME for? (b) When do I feel most useful? (c) Which 3 lessons from L1–L24 felt most "natural" already? Those are your core strengths.
2. **The values test — 10 minutes.** Write 3 non-negotiables ("I don't fake estimates", "I protect my team publicly", "I speak truth to the manager"). Then 3 things you would NEVER do for approval (connect to L22).
3. **The 24-tool audit — 15 minutes.** Go through all 24 lesson titles. Mark each: ✅ automatic / 🔧 still practicing / ✂️ not me (too heavy). Target: at least 6–8 automatic, no more than 3 🔧 at a time, and a clean conscience about the ✂️.
4. **The signature-moves card — 10 minutes.** Pick 3–4 moves. Write them as commands: "I clarify first, then commit." "I hold the silence." "Facts first, blame last." "I ask — you decide first." Read them aloud once a day.
5. **The style statement — 15 minutes, then rehearse.** One sentence: *"I lead by ___ because ___."* Say it 5 times aloud until it sounds like you, not a slogan.
