## 1. Core concept

**Pressure does one thing to your body: it speeds everything up — breath, heart, words.** The leader's skill under pressure is not *feeling* less; it's *moving slower on purpose*. Your calm is the room's calm.

The **pressure protocol** — four moves:

1. **Breathe first** (L2). The physiological sigh (double inhale + long exhale) — 3 seconds, invisible, before any words.
2. **Cut your speed 20%.** Under pressure, people rush. Deliberately speaking *slower than normal* is the single most powerful lever you have.
3. **Use the 3-frame structure** — the pressure version of P.R.E.A.:
   - *"Here's what we know."* (facts, no guesses)
   - *"Here's what we don't know."* (honesty — your credibility lives here)
   - *"Here's what I'm doing."* (the plan, with a time)
4. **Say the hard thing first.** Bad news buried in context reads as hiding; bad news first reads as courage. The trust you build in 10 honest seconds outlasts any spin.

**The unknown line:** *"I don't know — I'll have an answer by [time]."* This sentence alone separates leaders from bluffers. A timestamp follows "I don't know" every single time.

## 2. Why it matters

1. **Incidents, interviews, ambush questions — these moments define you.** Nobody remembers the calm Tuesday; they remember how you spoke when the database went down.
2. **Panic is contagious; so is calm.** Your pace sets the room's pace (L4 principle, in voice).
3. **Honesty under pressure builds permanent trust** — teams forgive bad news from a leader who gives it straight and then acts.
4. **For you specifically:** job interviews and production incidents are pressure arenas. This protocol is your armor for both.

## 4. Common mistakes

| Mistake | What it does | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Speaking faster when scared | Feeds panic (yours + theirs) | 20% slower |
| Over-explaining / rambling | Sounds guilty | The 3-frame |
| Burying the bad news | Reads as hiding | Hard thing first |
| Bluffing certainty ("it's fine!") | Trust destroyed when it breaks | "We don't know yet — here's the plan" |
| Hiding the unknown | Caught later, worse | "I don't know — answer by [time]" |
| Freezing / going silent for too long | Looks lost | The 3-frame IS your first sentence |
| Blaming (the vendor, the weather) | Leader = victim | Own it, fix it |

## 5. Workplace examples

- **Production incident (your world):** *"Here's what we know: the checkout API is down, 30% of traffic affected. What we don't know: the trigger — the last deploy is the prime suspect. What I'm doing: rollback in 10 minutes, and I'll report back every 15. Questions?"* (This one script covers it.)
- **Boss asks for the impossible deadline:** *"I can't honestly commit to Friday. Here's what we know: the migration needs four more test days. Here's my proposal: Wednesday, with the CSV export cut. What would change my mind: an extra pair of hands from the platform team."* (Hard thing first, alternative ready.)
- **Interview stumper (job search):** *"That's a good question — let me take a beat. What I know: I'd solve the read path with a cache. What I don't know yet: your write pattern. What I'd do: measure first, then cache."* (The pause + 3-frame is the answer.)
- **JP context:** under pressure, keep the same structure but even softer delivery: "I'm sorry for the delay — here is what we know…" (Apology once, then facts — never grovel repeatedly.)
- **Admitting a mistake to the team:** *"I made the call to cut testing, and it caused the incident. That's on me. Here's what I'm changing so it doesn't repeat."* (Own it → fix it → move on. L18's owning-decision skill under fire.)

## 6. Before vs After

| 😟 Panicked | 😌 Calm / confident |
|---|---|
| Talks at 200 wpm, breathless | 20% slower, steady (L3) |
| "I think maybe it's probably the cache or the deploy or…" | "Here's what we know / don't know / what I'm doing." |
| Hides the bad news, spins | Hard thing first, straight |
| "Everything's fine!!" | "It's serious — here's the plan." |
| "I don't know…" (frozen) | "I don't know — answer by 3pm." |

## 7. Practical drills (measurable)

1. **The 20%-slower drill — 10 min.** Record a 60-second update at your normal pace. Then record the same update at ~80% speed. Replay both — hear the difference in your own certainty. (Stopwatch: 60s vs 75s.)
2. **The 3-frame builder — 15 min.** Take a real current issue (the migration, a bug, a deadline risk). Write: what we know / don't know / what I'm doing. Say it in 45 seconds. ×3 topics.
3. **The hard-first drill — 10 min.** Record delivering bad news two ways: (a) softened with context first, (b) hard fact first, plan second. Feel the difference in your own authority.
4. **The timestamp reflex — 5 min.** Write 3 "I don't know" sentences, each with a timestamp: "I don't know — I'll have an answer by…". Say them aloud until the pattern is automatic.
5. **The cold-start practice — 5 min/day.** Every morning, answer this in 30 seconds, no preparation: *"What's the biggest risk to your sprint right now, and what are you doing about it?"*
