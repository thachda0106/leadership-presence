# Lesson 17 — Handling Criticism

> Program: Leadership Presence & Body Language · Phase 4 — Leadership Presence (5/8)
> Delivered: 2026-09-20 · Builds on: L2 (Breath), L12 (Listening), L10 (Silence), L5 (Eye Contact)

---

## 1. Core concept

**Criticism is data — not identity.** Someone says your work is wrong. That's information about the *work*. It is not a verdict about *you*. The leader's skill is keeping those two things separate while the adrenaline is still pumping.

The **receiving loop** — five moves:

1. **Pause.** One breath (L2). The instinct to defend fires in under a second; you deliberately let it pass.
2. **Listen fully.** No preparing your rebuttal while they talk (L12). Let them finish.
3. **Separate fact from tone.** "The migration is late" = fact. Laughed while saying it = tone. The fact stays; the tone has no power over you.
4. **Own what's true.** *"You're right — the estimate was wrong."* Owning the true part disarms the whole attack and raises your status.
5. **One question, then decide.** *"What would you like me to change first?"* → then a time-bounded answer. You don't have to agree with everything today.

**The leader's lines:** *"Thank you for telling me."* · *"Fair point — I'll check that."* · *"I hear you. What part worries you most?"*

## 2. Why it matters

1. **Defensiveness is the #1 status killer.** The instant you defend, you confirm the criticism matters and you shrink.
2. **Criticism is free consulting.** Someone just told you a blind spot — that's expensive information given away for free.
3. **How you receive criticism teaches your team how to give it.** If you melt down, people stop telling you the truth — and a leader without truth is flying blind.
4. **The pause alone wins most battles** — most criticism is delivered with heat; responding without heat makes you the calm one in the room (which you are).

## 3. Psychology / evidence

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| The brain treats status threats like physical threats (automatic defensiveness) | **Well-supported** (social-threat neuroscience) |
| Reappraisal — separating the message from the self — reduces the sting and improves outcomes | **Well-supported** (emotion-regulation research) |
| People who accept valid criticism learn faster | **Well-supported** (feedback studies, L16) |
| "Never show a reaction — ignore criticism completely" | **Wrong** — ignoring reads as arrogance; the skill is receiving, not pretending |
| "Over-apologizing shows humility" | **Wrong** — groveling collapses your status; own the fact, then move forward |

**A note on you (Thach):** criticism at work has historically stressed you (your background). This is exactly the skill that turns that stress into advantage — the pause and the fact/tone split are trainable, and they directly protect you.

## 4. Common mistakes

| Mistake | What it does | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Defending instantly | Confirms you're threatened | Pause + breath first |
| Counter-attacking ("what about YOUR bug?") | Escalation | Stay on the idea |
| Shutting down (silent + arms crossed) | Reads as sulking | Open posture (L1), keep listening |
| Over-apologizing ("I'm so sorry, I'm terrible") | Collapses status | Own the fact once, then plan |
| Dismissing ("I know, I know") | Arrogant | "Fair point — let me check that." |
| Ruminating all day | Wasted energy | Fact vs tone separation, then let it go |
| Taking JP indirect criticism literally | Misses the message | Listen for the implied critique |

## 5. Workplace examples

- **Code review rejection:** "The reviewer said the design is overcomplicated." → Pause. *"Fair — the abstraction layer might be more than we need. Let me rework it and come back with a simpler version Thursday."* (Data, no drama.)
- **Manager on a missed estimate:** *"You're right — I underestimated the migration. Here's what I know now, here's my correction, and here's the new date."* (Own → plan → commit.)
- **JP indirect criticism:** the manager says *"That approach might be difficult in this environment."* That IS the criticism. Response: *"I see. Let me reconsider — would you have time to look at my next version?"* (Hear it, respond respectfully, never argue openly.)
- **Interview (job search):** *"Your architecture is over-engineered for this scale."* → Pause, *"That's a fair challenge — I optimized for growth. Walk me through the scale you expect, and I'll adjust."* (This answer alone wins interviews.)
- **Public criticism in a meeting:** breathe, *"You're right about the delay. Let me answer the impact question now and take the rest offline."* (Calm, structured, doesn't feed the room's heat.)

## 6. Before vs After

| 😟 Defensive | 😌 Calm / confident |
|---|---|
| "That's not true! We did everything right!" | Pause. "Fair point — the estimate was wrong." |
| Counter-attacks | Stays on the idea |
| Arms crossed, silent, hurt | Open posture, listening (L12) |
| "I'm so sorry, I'm the worst" | Owns the fact once, then the plan |
| Carries it for days | Fact vs tone, then moves on |
| Misses the JP hint, argues literally | Hears the implied critique, responds softly |

## 7. Practical drills (measurable)

1. **The 3-breath pause — all day.** Every time you feel defensive today: 3 breaths before ANY reply. Count how many times you caught the spike.
2. **The fact/tone journal — 10 min.** Write down 3 recent criticisms. Split each into two columns: FACT (what's true) and TONE (how it was said). Train your eye to find the data in the noise.
3. **The rewrite drill — 10 min.** Take 3 defensive replies you actually made (or wanted to make). Rewrite each as: Pause → own the true part → one question. Say them aloud.
4. **The gratitude line — 5 min.** Say *"Thank you for telling me"* 5 times in a row until it feels natural. It sounds strange at first — that's the point of practicing.
5. **The 60-second receive — 10 min.** Record yourself responding to: *"Your PR was rejected — the error handling is a mess."* Replay: where did your eyes go? Did you defend? (Record one more version, calmer.)
