# Lesson 5 — Eye Contact

> Program: Leadership Presence & Body Language · Phase 2 — Connection (1/4)
> Delivered: 2026-09-18 · Builds on: Phase 1 (Posture, Breathing, Voice, Walking)

---

## 1. Core concept

**Eye contact is a handshake with your eyes.** Hold too long = crushing the hand (staring, aggressive). Never look = refusing to shake (uncertain, hiding). Natural = contact, hold briefly, look away, come back — a **rhythm**, not a hold.

Three practical tools:

| Tool | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The 50/70 guideline** | Look at the other person ~**50% of the time while speaking**, ~**70% while listening** |
| **The triangle technique** | Move your gaze between their left eye → right eye → mouth. A small shift every 2–4 seconds = relaxed, warm, never a stare |
| **The group sweep** | In meetings/presentations: give **2–4 seconds** to each person, in a deliberate rotation — includes everyone, excludes no one |

**Mental model:** the eye sends a signal *"I see you, I'm with you, I'm confident."* A calm leader uses brief, warm, moving eye contact — not a laser stare.

## 2. Why it matters

1. **Trust and credibility** — people trust those who look at them when speaking *and* listening.
2. **Confidence signal** — looking down = submission/uncertainty; meeting eyes = composure.
3. **It keeps YOU present** — eyes that wander = mind that wanders, and others feel it.
4. **You read the room with your eyes** — reactions, confusion, agreement.
5. **Inclusion (a leader's gesture)** — distributing gaze in a group says *"everyone here matters."*

## 3. Psychology / evidence

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| More eye contact → rated more competent, credible, trustworthy | **Well-supported** (many perception studies) |
| Mutual gaze increases liking and cooperation | **Well-supported** |
| Gaze patterns help manage turn-taking in conversation | **Well-supported** |
| The 50/70 rule, triangle, 2–4s holds, 3–5s group sweeps | **Reasonable practical advice** — coaching heuristics, not measured laws. Great targets, don't count them religiously |
| "Liars avoid eye contact — eye contact proves honesty" | **Popular myth — limited evidence.** Detectives' folk wisdom; liars sometimes make MORE eye contact to appear honest. Don't use gaze as a lie detector |
| "Always hold eye contact to show strength" | **Wrong** — sustained unbroken gaze reads as staring/aggression, and it's exhausting |
| "Stare at the bridge of the nose" | **Wrong** — that IS a stare. The triangle *moves* |

**Culture — critical for your context (Japanese enterprise):** sustained eye contact is NOT universal. In Japan, prolonged direct gaze can read as aggressive or rude — subordinates often lower their gaze toward superiors as respect. So: with Japanese managers, use **brief, calm eye contact + respectful breaks + a nod** — not the Western full-hold. With your team and peers, normal warm contact is fine. When in doubt, notice what the other person does and mirror *their* comfortable amount.

**Also:** some people avoid eye contact for many reasons (neurodivergence, anxiety, culture). Read behavior with context — never conclude from one signal (this is the program's core principle).

## 4. Common mistakes

| Mistake | What it does | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Staring without breaking | Aggressive, uncomfortable | 2–4s holds, then a natural break |
| Looking down when speaking | Submissive, shy | Meet eyes at the START of your sentence |
| Darting eyes | Nervous, untrustworthy | Slow, deliberate gaze changes |
| Looking at laptop/screen while they speak | Disrespect, disengaged | Look at the speaker while listening |
| Fixing on ONE person in a group | Excludes the rest | The group sweep |
| On video: looking at the screen, not the camera | "Eye contact" misses you | Look at the LENS when it's your turn |
| Forcing long looks at a Japanese superior | Discomfort, disrespect | Brief gaze + nod + respectful breaks |

## 5. Workplace examples

- **Stand-up (room or camera):** sweep the group — 2–3 seconds per person while you speak.
- **1-on-1 with a team member:** lean on the **70% while listening** — it tells them you truly hear them.
- **Interview:** meet the interviewer when you START an answer; break naturally mid-thought (looking up while thinking is fine); return to hold the close of your answer.
- **Disagreeing:** steady, calm eye contact says *"I'm certain, and I'm not threatened"* — but never a stare.
- **Video call:** look at the **camera lens** when speaking (dot a sticker beside the lens if you keep drifting); look at the screen while listening.
- **Japanese manager context:** brief eye contact, then a respectful break with a nod — calm, polite, in-culture.
- **Presenting to a room:** one person per point — hold each face 2–4 seconds as you deliver each idea; never talk to the slides.

## 6. Before vs After

| 😟 Nervous version | 😌 Calm / confident version |
|---|---|
| Drops eyes instantly when asked a question | Meets eyes, holds 2–4s, breaks naturally |
| Looks at floor/laptop while speaking | Looks AT people while speaking |
| Darting, scanning everywhere | Slow, deliberate gaze changes |
| Talks to the slides / screen | Talks to faces |
| On camera: stares at the preview | Looks at the lens |
| Zero gaze at superiors (JP) | Brief eye contact + nod, then respectful break |

## 7. Practical drills (measurable)

1. **The 4-1 rhythm — 2 min.** Opposite a photo face (or a person who's game): hold gaze **4 seconds (count in your head), break 1 second, return**. ×10. This builds the natural rhythm into muscle memory.
2. **The triangle tour — 1 min × 3.** On a photo: cycle left eye → right eye → mouth, ~2s each, for 1 minute. Feel how "warm" it becomes vs a fixed stare.
3. **The 50/70 count — one conversation.** With a willing friend/partner or on a recorded call: consciously count — "speaking: was I looking ~half the time? listening: ~70%?" Just noticing changes it.
4. **The group sweep — 5 min.** Place 3 chairs/objects around the room. Deliver your stand-up opening 3 times, giving each "person" one sentence (2–3s each), rotating left → center → right.
5. **The lens drill — 10 min.** Put a small dot/sticker on your screen next to the camera lens. Record 90 seconds of an answer while looking at the dot (the lens). Watch the replay: were your eyes on the dot, or drifting to the preview/screen?
6. **The listening hold — all day.** In every conversation today, when the OTHER person is talking, hold a full **5-second gaze** at them at least 3 times. Count how many you remember.

## 8. Real-world challenge (today)
